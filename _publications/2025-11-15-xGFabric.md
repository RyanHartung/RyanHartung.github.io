## Abstract

Many scientific applications need to connect geographically distributed sensors with the computing power of high-performance computing (HPC) facilities. In digital agriculture, Citrus Under Protective Screening (CUPS) uses sensors to monitor environmental conditions and detect damage to protective screens. Researchers can use computational fluid dynamics simulations to understand those conditions and inform timely interventions, such as watering or robotic repairs.

Connecting these systems is challenging: sensor networks can have limited capacity and unreliable data access, while HPC systems offer substantial computing power but commonly use high-latency batch processing. This paper presents xGFabric, an end-to-end system that uses private 5G networking to connect remote sensors to HPC facilities. Its prototype uses 5G network slicing to support near-real-time digital-agriculture simulations.
