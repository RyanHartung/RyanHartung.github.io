I am a Ph.D. student in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Notre Dame, where I work with [Dr. Douglas Thain](https://cse.nd.edu/faculty/douglas-thain/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}. My research is at the intersection of high-performance computing, distributed systems, and edge computing.

I build systems that connect sensor networks and edge devices with remote HPC resources, so data-driven applications can respond quickly while computationally intensive simulations and model updates run in the background. A central application for this work is digital agriculture: for example, using sensor data and computational fluid dynamics to understand conditions in citrus screenhouses. I am part of the [xGFabric project](https://sites.google.com/view/xgfabric){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}, which uses private 5G to connect agricultural sensors with HPC facilities, and my recent work explores asynchronous workflows and low-latency inference across the edge–HPC continuum.

Outside this research, I enjoy building software projects, including tools in Rust and Linux. You can learn more about my work on the [projects](/projects/), [publications](/publications/), [posters](/posters/), and [talks](/talks/) pages.
